TOMÁS FERNÁNDEZ VÉRTIZ
SELECTED WORKS — FILM
Tomás Fernández Vértiz is a filmmaker and photographer from Mexico City. Trained at Goldsmiths, University of London, his work moves between Mexico, Spain and the UK, drawn to places and the people who inhabit them. Working across documentary film and photography, his projects attend to landscape, memory and the quiet traces people leave behind.

“El Sentir de las Montañas” (The Feel of the Mountains) is a work made with members of the Latin American community in London. The film is divided in 3 chapters, through which we meet different people. Trying to understand more about some of the different situations that Latin American people have experienced or are experiencing in London, the film is an exploration of arrival, community and healing. Through the work we hear the story of an arrest, we meet a volleyball playing community and take a look at an alternative therapy. Winner of the Warden's Art Prize (Goldsmiths, University of London). Screened at Waking Life Festival: Moon Cinema 2023 (Portugal) and at the London Migration Film Festival 2022, Catford Mews, with a director Q&A.
The first chapter opens with a series of London landscapes before settling into Jorge's kitchen. While he prepares a meal, he tells us about his experience of arriving in the United Kingdom from Mexico.
Chapter 1: Jorge
The second chapter follows an Ecuadorian community that gathers to play Ecuavolley, their own national version of the sport — creating community through sport, but also asking what makes one part of a new community, and how time shapes a sense of belonging in the face of melancholia.
Chapter 2: Ecuavolley
The third chapter stays with Claudia, who arrived from Colombia. Through her practice of alternative therapies, the film observes care as a way of healing. The conversations with Claudia look at migration within the context of dealing with pain and dwelling.
Chapter 3: Claudia

DOS NOCHES Y TRES DÍAS
Two-channel documentary · 20 min · 2021 · Chinampas, Mexico
A two-channel documentary filmed in Chinampas, a community in central Mexico from which several of its members have had to migrate to the United States. The film observes the relationship between the community and the nature that surrounds it — always in the presence of a wind farm — and asks where the future of communities affected by poverty lies, in a context defined by environmental progress, violence and corruption. Presented at APT Gallery and Fitzrovia Gallery (London), the London Migration Film Festival (2021) and Waking Life Festival: Moon Cinema (Portugal, 2022).
With barely any dialogue, the film follows members of the Chinampas community in their daily tasks — a music class, women baking bread, cowboys fixing gear and herding bulls. The film aims to show that quiet observation can sometimes say more than words.
Always in the presence of Palo Alto — a wind farm of 43 turbines that towers over the village — the film contrasts the promise of environmental progress with what the industry actually brings to the community: few opportunities for the locals, and none of the electricity for the town itself.
The lack of opportunities and the increasing violence in the region have led many members of the community to migrate to the United States in search of a better life; some have left and returned several times. The film simply contemplates them, as they too wait.
BAJO EL MISMO SOL
BAJO EL MISMO SOL
Documentary · In post-production · Galicia, Spain · Director / Editor
An observational documentary filmed in rural Galicia, Spain. Shot across the life of a small village — its fields, labours and festivals — the film weaves everything it gathers into a single imagined day, from first light to nightfall. Currently in post-production, edited as a proof-of-concept for a feature-length film.
